FIRST, THE GROWN-UP

Set up your family.

01

Sign in with your email.

Choose Sign in on the home page. Enter your parent email and choose Email me a sign-in link. Open the email, follow its link, then confirm Sign in.

You’ll arrive at Your family in the lab. There’s no password to create.

Five minutes, one use. Parent email links expire after five minutes. If yours expires or has already been used, request another one.
Open parent sign-in ↗
02

Create a profile for each child.

In Your family, open Add a child. Choose a nickname and an icon, then select Create child profile. Repeat for each learner.

Each child gets a separate profile and saved lesson progress. Kids don’t need their own email address or password.

03

Make a kid login link.

On the child’s card, choose Make a 5-minute login link. Use Copy link to share it, or let your child scan the QR code with their device.

The link uses friendly words and a random ending. It works once. Making a new link replaces the previous unused link for that child.

The lesson doesn’t end after five minutes. That’s the time available to use the link. Once your child signs in, they can keep building after the link expires.
04

Open it on the child’s device.

Open the link and choose Open my lab. Check that the child’s nickname appears at the top of the lab before they begin.

Use the child’s device or a separate browser profile if you want to keep your parent dashboard open. Tabs in the same browser share a sign-in: opening a child link in your parent browser switches that browser to the child.

Sharing one computer? To return to the parent view, sign out of the child profile, then sign in again with your parent email.

NOW, THE BUILDER

Build the first circuit.

Open the lab from the child’s profile so their progress is saved to them.

05

Find the parts.

The first lesson starts with a checklist for the official Arduino Uno R3 Starter Kit:

  • 1 classic Arduino Uno R3
  • 1 USB-A to USB-B cable
  • 1 breadboard, 400 points
  • 1 red, two-legged LED
  • 1 resistor, 220 Ω
  • 2 solid-core jumper wires

Choose a part’s name to see a close-up. Tick off each part, then choose My parts are ready! No kit yet? You can identify the parts on screen and use the virtual workbench.

06

Follow each animated step.

Watch where the part or wire goes, then use Next to continue. Watch that again replays the placement.

Rotate
Drag with your mouse or one finger.
Move
Choose Move, then drag. Shift-drag also works. On touch screens, use two fingers.
Zoom
Scroll, pinch, or use the + and − buttons.
Inspect
Click a part or pin. Choose Back to this step, close the note, or press Escape to return.
Reset view
Use the target button to see the whole diagram again.

Choose Build + Code, Build only, Code only, or Guided above the workbench. Your layout choice is saved in that browser.

For a hands-on practice tour, choose App tour at the top. It keeps your circuit lesson’s progress separate.

07

Change the blink timing.

In the guided code editor, change Blink delay. Slow, Steady, and Fast change both wait instructions in the program.

Stuck? Ask Sparky. Tap the hedgehog under the code, then Give me a hint to highlight the two wait lines. Show a faster blink or Show a slower blink previews a change. Choose Try it to apply it, or Undo Sparky’s change to restore your old waits. Close × hides the help. Sparky uses prepared hints for this lesson; he does not chat or add arbitrary code. Run a fresh test after any change.

// Shorter waits make a faster blink.
digitalWrite(13, HIGH);
delay(500);
digitalWrite(13, LOW);
delay(500);

This editor uses timing controls rather than free typing. LED color changes the virtual LED only. Copy code copies the matching sketch for use in the Arduino IDE. Changing the timing clears the previous test result.

08

Run the virtual test.

In the final lesson step, choose Test my circuit. Little Spark runs the compiled program on a virtual Arduino and checks the LED timing.

Wait for Test passed under the code. The LED starts blinking, and the final step is marked complete. Pause the light stops the animation.

Watch the save status. Your child’s changes sync to their profile. Wait for Progress synced before switching devices. If a message asks you to reload after an account change or reset, use its Reload button.

BACK TO THE PARENT

See how it’s going.

09

Open your Family dashboard.

In your parent browser, choose Family. Each child’s card shows completed steps, a progress bar, and the last save time. Close and reopen Family to fetch the latest progress.

To continue on another device, make a fresh login link for the same child profile. Their saved progress follows that profile.

Under Edit profile, change the child’s nickname or icon. Sign out this child’s devices revokes their current sessions and unused login link. Their profile and saved progress remain.

Parent practice is different. If you explore the lesson while signed in as a parent, that practice stays on your browser. Use the child’s profile for progress you want to track on their card.

OPTIONAL · WITH A GROWN-UP

Try a real Uno.

10

Check, connect, and upload.

Build the circuit with USB unplugged. Have a grown-up check the LED legs, 220 Ω resistor, and wires against the lesson.

Pass the virtual test, then open Connect a board. Confirm the wiring check, plug in the classic Uno R3, choose Connect Uno, and then Send tested code.

Direct USB upload needs a computer browser with Web Serial, such as Chrome or Edge. In Safari, use Download sketch for Arduino IDE and upload through the Arduino IDE.

Program test ≠ wiring check. The emulator can’t inspect real wires. The uploader is for a classic Uno R3 / ATmega328P; Uno R4, Nano, and ESP32 aren’t supported by this lesson. Physical uploads still need a hands-on hardware check.

ANOTHER FIRST PROJECT / MAKEY MAKEY CLASSIC

Make a fruit piano.

11

Choose Fruit Piano in the lab.

The field guide links to both lessons. Fruit Piano has seven steps, its own unboxing checklist, an animated connection diagram, and a playable browser instrument. A child’s steps save separately from Blink an LED; parents see each lesson in Family. Parent practice stays in this browser.

  1. Unbox: find one Makey Makey Classic, its USB data cable, and four alligator leads. Bring a computer, three bananas (or three separate foil pads), and a dry surface from home. Fruit is not in the kit. Use a USB adapter if your computer needs one.
  2. Add USB: use the supplied cable to connect the board to your computer. It acts like a keyboard; there is no code to upload.
  3. Connect EARTH: clip a lead to the front EARTH strip. Hold the bare metal at the other end.
  4. Try SPACE: while holding EARTH, touch the board’s SPACE pad with your other hand. In the diagram, Connect virtual EARTH and Try SPACE show the closed loop. Release virtual EARTH to see the open loop.
  5. First fruit: unplug USB while moving clips. Join SPACE to one banana or foil pad with a second lead.
  6. Two more keys: join LEFT to the second fruit and UP to the third. Keep fruits and metal clips apart. Reconnect USB when ready.
  7. Play: in step 7, choose Virtual practice, connect virtual EARTH, and tap the three keys. Or choose Real board / keyboard, Sound on, then Focus piano. Hold the real EARTH clip and touch each fruit. SPACE plays C, LEFT plays D, and UP plays E. Play all three notes to complete the final step. Mute keeps the visual flashes.

Tap a part for a closer look and a note; Back to diagram resets the view. + and − zoom, the arrow buttons move the diagram, and Reset view brings it back. Watch that again replays the connection animation. The Fruit Piano App tour explains these controls without completing steps. Reset this lesson resets only Fruit Piano.

If no note plays, click Sound on and Focus piano again. Keep this tab active. Try your computer keyboard’s Space, Left and Up keys. Check USB, EARTH, and clip contact; keep food away from the computer. This visualization is not an electronics emulator, and keyboard input cannot confirm real wiring or identify a Makey Makey.

AFTER BLINK / ARDUINO UNO R3

Build three traffic lights.

12

Choose Traffic Lights in the field guide.

Start with the unboxing checklist: one Uno R3, USB-A to USB-B cable, 400-point breadboard, one red/yellow/green LED, three 220 Ω resistors, and six solid-core jumper wires. All wiring below is done with USB unplugged.

  1. Explore the board: D13 controls red, D12 yellow, D11 green.
  2. Set out the breadboard. Each A–E column group connects underneath; F–J is a separate group.
  3. Place red long leg in E4, short leg F4; yellow E13/F13; green E22/F22.
  4. Add a separate 220 Ω resistor from C4 to C10, C13 to C19, and C22 to C28.
  5. Wire D13 to A10, D12 to A19, and D11 to A28. Join GND to H4, J4 to J13, and I13 to J22. Watch the six paths appear. Match pin and hole names even if your wire colors differ.
  6. In step 7, predict the next color, then choose Test my sequence. The actual compiled Uno program must show red → green → yellow, with only one light at a time. Red and green each last twice as long as yellow.

Sequence pace offers Slow (2 s red, 2 s green, 1 s yellow), Steady (1 s, 1 s, ½ s), and Fast (½ s, ½ s, ¼ s). Ask Sparky explains HIGH/LOW and previews a faster or slower sequence. Try it applies all three waits; Undo restores the old pace. Every pace change, including Undo, requires a new test before upload. Pause stops the lights.

The 3D controls, close-up/back controls, four layouts and App tour work as in Blink. Traffic Lights saves separately on child profiles and appears as its own row in Family. Reset lesson progress affects only this lesson.

For the real model, pass the virtual test and have a grown-up check all three LED directions, resistors and six wires. Connect a classic Uno R3 and send the exact tested program using desktop Chrome or Edge. Download sketch exports the matching program for Arduino IDE. Physical hardware still needs hands-on validation. This lesson is a model, not a road-signal controller.

MAKEY MAKEY / MATERIALS

Conductivity Detective.

13

Predict, test, and record.

Choose Conductivity Detective in the field guide. Find a Makey Makey Classic, USB data cable, two alligator leads, computer, dry work surface, and four samples from home: kitchen foil, banana, dry plastic, and dry wood. The samples are not kit contents.

  1. Connect USB with a grown-up. Connect one lead to EARTH and hold the bare metal.
  2. With USB unplugged while moving clips, join the other lead to SPACE and one sample. Reconnect USB and touch the sample while holding EARTH.
  3. In step 5, choose a sample in the notebook and I predict yes or I predict no. Virtual practice needs Connect virtual EARTH before Test sample. Its results are prepared examples, not measurements of real materials.
  4. For your own kit, choose Real board / keyboard and Focus activity. SPACE records Key received. If no key arrives, choose Record no key. A normal keyboard can send SPACE too. The app cannot identify the board or prove that your wiring is correct.
  5. Check the tester with foil before interpreting a no-key result. Contact, moisture, coating and setup can affect real outcomes. Record the result you observe even when it differs from your prediction or the virtual example.
  6. Predict and test all four samples. Step 7 completes when all four records exist; a wrong prediction does not block completion. The notebook records prediction, result and virtual/real mode, and saves to the child profile.

Use +/−, move arrows, Reset view, Replay and part notes to explore. Ask Sparky explains the tester; tap again to hide the help. Reset this lesson clears only this notebook and lesson. Keep samples and moisture away from your computer; use only the board’s USB connection.

ARDUINO / INPUT → OUTPUT

Push-button Light.

14

Let a button control an LED.

Find the Uno R3, USB cable, breadboard, one red LED, one four-legged momentary pushbutton, one 220 Ω resistor and four jumper wires. Leave USB unplugged while building.

  1. Place the LED long leg E11, short leg F11. Put the button legs in E22/F22/E24/F24 with each always-connected pair in one numbered column. Have a grown-up check the pairs and orientation; rotating a four-leg switch can make it behave differently.
  2. Add the LED resistor C11–C17. Wire D13–A17 and GND–J11.
  3. Wire D2–A22 and J24–I11. This joins the opposite button pair to the existing ground group. INPUT_PULLUP supplies the input’s internal pull-up; the LED still needs its external 220 Ω resistor.
  4. Predict what the LED will do. In step 7 choose Test my button. The compiled Uno program is checked with released, pressed, and released inputs.
  5. After a pass, Press virtual button holds the input down; Release virtual button lets it go. This controls the emulator’s D2 pin. A real switch is momentary: hold and release it on your desk.
  6. Button rule selects Press = on or Press = off. The input still reads LOW when pressed; only the output rule changes. Sparky previews the digitalWrite line, Try it applies, and Undo restores the previous rule. Test again after any rule change.

Use the same 3D camera, close-up/back controls and four layouts as Blink. A grown-up must check real wiring and button orientation before upload. Send tested code uses exactly the verified image on a classic Uno R3; Download sketch exports that rule for Arduino IDE. Physical board behavior still needs a hands-on test. MAKEY MAKEY / BUILD A CONTROLLER

Game Controller.

15

Four foil arrows. Three sparks.

Find the Makey Makey Classic, USB data cable, five alligator leads, computer, and four foil pads with cardboard and tape from home.

  1. Connect USB and EARTH as in Fruit Piano. Hold the metal EARTH clip.
  2. With USB unplugged while moving clips, tape four separate foil pads to dry cardboard. Join LEFT to one pad, then UP, RIGHT and DOWN to the other three. Keep foil and metal clips apart; reconnect USB when ready.
  3. In step 6 choose Virtual practice and Connect virtual EARTH, then use the on-screen arrows. For your real controller choose Real board / keyboard and Focus activity. Hold EARTH and touch a foil arrow. Ordinary keyboard arrows also work.
  4. Each distinct press moves one square on a five-by-five board. There is no timer. In step 7 collect all three sparks to complete the lesson.
  5. New round restarts the game while keeping the lesson’s completed steps. The child profile saves current position and collected sparks. Reset this lesson clears both the round and this lesson’s progress, preserving other lessons.

If the page scrolls instead, Focus activity again. Keyboard shortcuts are handled only while that activity region has focus. Ask Sparky explains the foil controls. The connection diagram is a teaching picture and keyboard input cannot verify your real board or wiring.

QUICK HELP

If you get stuck.

The login link expired.+

Parents can request a new email link from Sign in. For a child, open the parent’s Family dashboard and make a new five-minute link for that profile. Each link works once.

The wrong nickname is at the top.+

Sign out of that profile. Ask the parent to make a link for the correct child, and open it on the intended device. A second tab in the same browser shares the current sign-in.

I can’t see my child’s progress.+

Check that the child is building under their own nickname. Wait for Progress synced in their lab, then close and reopen Family in your parent browser. Steps are marked complete as the learner advances; the last step completes after the virtual test passes.

The sign-in email hasn’t arrived.+

Check the address and your spam or junk folder. If the link expires, return to Sign in and request a new one. Avoid requesting several links at once: only the newest email link works.
