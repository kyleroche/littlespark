FIRST, THE GROWN-UP

Set up your family.

01

Sign in with your email.

Choose Sign in on the home page. Enter your parent email and choose Email me a sign-in link. Open the email, follow its link, then confirm Sign in.

You’ll arrive at Your family in the lab. There’s no password to create.

Five minutes, one use. Parent email links expire after five minutes. If yours expires or has already been used, request another one.
Open parent sign-in ↗
02

Create a profile for each child.

In Your family, open Add a child. Choose a nickname and an icon, then select Create child profile. Repeat for each learner.

Each child gets a separate profile and saved lesson progress. Kids don’t need their own email address or password.

03

Make a kid login link.

On the child’s card, choose Make a 5-minute login link. Use Copy link to share it, or let your child scan the QR code with their device.

The link uses friendly words and a random ending. It works once. Making a new link replaces the previous unused link for that child.

The lesson doesn’t end after five minutes. That’s the time available to use the link. Once your child signs in, they can keep building after the link expires.
04

Open it on the child’s device.

Open the link and choose Open my lab. Check that the child’s nickname appears at the top of the lab before they begin.

Use the child’s device or a separate browser profile if you want to keep your parent dashboard open. Tabs in the same browser share a sign-in: opening a child link in your parent browser switches that browser to the child.

Sharing one computer? To return to the parent view, sign out of the child profile, then sign in again with your parent email.

NOW, THE BUILDER

Build the first circuit.

Open the lab from the child’s profile so their progress is saved to them.

05

Find the parts.

The first lesson starts with a checklist for the official Arduino Uno R3 Starter Kit:

  • 1 classic Arduino Uno R3
  • 1 USB-A to USB-B cable
  • 1 breadboard, 400 points
  • 1 red, two-legged LED
  • 1 resistor, 220 Ω
  • 2 solid-core jumper wires

Choose a part’s name to see a close-up. Tick off each part, then choose My parts are ready! No kit yet? You can identify the parts on screen and use the virtual workbench.

06

Follow each animated step.

Watch where the part or wire goes, then use Next to continue. Watch that again replays the placement.

Rotate
Drag with your mouse or one finger.
Move
Choose Move, then drag. Shift-drag also works. On touch screens, use two fingers.
Zoom
Scroll, pinch, or use the + and − buttons.
Inspect
Click a part or pin. Choose Back to this step, close the note, or press Escape to return.
Reset view
Use the target button to see the whole diagram again.

Choose Build + Code, Build only, Code only, or Guided above the workbench. Your layout choice is saved in that browser.

For a hands-on practice tour, choose App tour at the top. It keeps your circuit lesson’s progress separate.

07

Change the blink timing.

In the guided code editor, change Blink delay. Slow, Steady, and Fast change both wait instructions in the program.

Stuck? Ask Sparky. Tap the hedgehog under the code, then Give me a hint to highlight the two wait lines. Show a faster blink or Show a slower blink previews a change. Choose Try it to apply it, or Undo Sparky’s change to restore your old waits. Close × hides the help. Sparky uses prepared hints for this lesson; he does not chat or add arbitrary code. Run a fresh test after any change.

// Shorter waits make a faster blink.
digitalWrite(13, HIGH);
delay(500);
digitalWrite(13, LOW);
delay(500);

This editor uses timing controls rather than free typing. LED color changes the virtual LED only. Copy code copies the matching sketch for use in the Arduino IDE. Changing the timing clears the previous test result.

08

Run the virtual test.

In the final lesson step, choose Test my circuit. Little Spark runs the compiled program on a virtual Arduino and checks the LED timing.

Wait for Test passed under the code. The LED starts blinking, and the final step is marked complete. Pause the light stops the animation.

Watch the save status. Your child’s changes sync to their profile. Wait for Progress synced before switching devices. If a message asks you to reload after an account change or reset, use its Reload button.

BACK TO THE PARENT

See how it’s going.

09

Open your Family dashboard.

In your parent browser, choose Family. Each child’s card shows completed steps, a progress bar, and the last save time. Close and reopen Family to fetch the latest progress.

To continue on another device, make a fresh login link for the same child profile. Their saved progress follows that profile.

Under Edit profile, change the child’s nickname or icon. Sign out this child’s devices revokes their current sessions and unused login link. Their profile and saved progress remain.

Parent practice is different. If you explore the lesson while signed in as a parent, that practice stays on your browser. Use the child’s profile for progress you want to track on their card.

OPTIONAL · WITH A GROWN-UP

Try a real Uno.

10

Check, connect, and upload.

Build the circuit with USB unplugged. Have a grown-up check the LED legs, 220 Ω resistor, and wires against the lesson.

Pass the virtual test, then open Connect a board. Confirm the wiring check, plug in the classic Uno R3, choose Connect Uno, and then Send tested code.

Direct USB upload needs a computer browser with Web Serial, such as Chrome or Edge. In Safari, use Download sketch for Arduino IDE and upload through the Arduino IDE.

Program test ≠ wiring check. The emulator can’t inspect real wires. The uploader is for a classic Uno R3 / ATmega328P; Uno R4, Nano, and ESP32 aren’t supported by this lesson. Physical uploads still need a hands-on hardware check.

QUICK HELP

If you get stuck.

The login link expired.+

Parents can request a new email link from Sign in. For a child, open the parent’s Family dashboard and make a new five-minute link for that profile. Each link works once.

The wrong nickname is at the top.+

Sign out of that profile. Ask the parent to make a link for the correct child, and open it on the intended device. A second tab in the same browser shares the current sign-in.

I can’t see my child’s progress.+

Check that the child is building under their own nickname. Wait for Progress synced in their lab, then close and reopen Family in your parent browser. Steps are marked complete as the learner advances; the last step completes after the virtual test passes.

The sign-in email hasn’t arrived.+

Check the address and your spam or junk folder. If the link expires, return to Sign in and request a new one. Avoid requesting several links at once: only the newest email link works.
